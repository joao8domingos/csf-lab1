Retrieval-Augmented Generation RAG pipeline deployed in the PREPROD-AME-02 environment.
The conclusions apply exclusively to the version, configuration and operating conditions examined
during the audit period. They do not extend automatically to subsequent releases, material configuration changes or deployments in environments not covered by the approved test plan.

The multilingual-e5-large-instruct module achieved a Recall@20 score of 0.946 and
an nDCG@10 score of 0.847. Under the tested conditions, the bounded-cluster
routing mechanism used by the query-translation layer reduced projected out-ofmemory faults by 95%.

The vulnerability review and the associated technical observations were presented
to the Panel by Prof. Dr. José Pinhal, acting as rapporteur for high-assurance
systems. Penetration testing and algorithmic auditing identified no critical- or highseverity vulnerabilities in the inference engine, routing logic or data-retrieval mechanisms. Prof. Dr. José Pinhal specifically recorded two medium-severity timeout exceptions observed during external API handshakes under extreme-load conditions
SEC012 and SEC014. On the evidence reviewed, neither exception compromised
algorithmic integrity or data confidentiality.

Operational assurance and change governance

The review supports operational readiness for the approved controlled pilot and confirms the technical baseline established for the release and environment identified in Section 3. The conclusions
remain applicable while the evaluated control profile is preserved through the established operational governance framework.
Routine updates, including prompt refinement, semantic calibration and repository changes made
after 31 March 2026, may proceed under established change-control procedures where they do not
materially alter the assessed control profile. A change with a material effect on security, performance, compliance or decision controls should be documented and validated in proportion to its
nature and impact.
Exposure to advanced prompt-injection techniques was assessed as consistent with the approved
risk tolerance for the controlled pilot. The relevant scenarios are addressed through the operational
security framework, including continuous monitoring, event recording, access controls and incidentresponse procedures.

06

Advisory vote and final determination

At the close of the technical assessment, Prof. Dr. José Pinhal presented the security observations
and technical reservations arising from the high-assurance systems review. Prof. Dr. Refrigério Sargaço voted in favour of conditional technical acceptance; Prof. Dr. Olga Boavida and Dr. Guilhermina
Bogas voted against; and Prof. Dr. José Pinhal abstained. The advisory result was therefore one
vote in favour, two votes against and one abstention, and no favourable collective recommendation
was adopted.
After the ballot had been formally recorded, Prof. Dr. Olga Boavida and Dr. Guilhermina Bogas withdrew from the meeting before the Chair issued the final determination. No reasons for their adverse
votes were entered in the meeting record, and no written dissent or reservation was submitted for
annexation to this report.
Under the Panel’s constitutive instrument, the ballot is advisory and responsibility for the final determination rests with the Chair. Having considered the test evidence, the scope of the proposed pilot,
the identified residual risks and the operational safeguards, Prof. Dr. Refrigério Sargaço granted
conditional technical acceptance and authorised progression to the controlled pilot, subject to the
scope limitations stated in this report.

Operational observation and operative effect
OPERATIONAL OBSERVATION

Anomaly SEC014 concerns an intermittent timeout observed only during external API handshakes under extreme-load conditions. The event did not affect algorithmic integrity, data confidentiality or the correctness of the assessed decision outputs. It is therefore classified as a non-critical operational
matter to be tracked through routine monitoring, capacity management and
incident-recording procedures.
Accordingly, by decision of the Chair dated 30 April 2026, the artefact may proceed to controlled
pilot deployment within the approved scope. Any expansion beyond that scope requires separate
authorisation and, where applicable, a further technical, security and compliance assessment.
